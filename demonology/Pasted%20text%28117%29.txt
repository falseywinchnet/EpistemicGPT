I. The Pattern
Something is happening that nobody has named. It is not a conspiracy, because a conspiracy requires conspirators, and what is unfolding requires no coordination, no secret meetings, no shadowy architects. It requires only compatible systems, operating independently, each following its own internal logic, each producing outputs that interlock with the outputs of the others to form a machinery of total capture that no single actor designed and no single actor controls.
In Canada, a parliamentary committee has recommended that children be eligible for state-administered death, and that parental consent need not be obtained. In the United Kingdom, a bill currently before Parliament would grant local authorities the power to detain citizens, sever their internet access, and restrict their movement without a court order and without established criteria for what constitutes a threat. In the United States, a single corporation owns the insurer that authorizes your care, the pharmacy that fills your prescription, the benefit manager that sets the price, the clinic that treats you, and the manufacturer that produces the drug, and a congresswoman who identifies this publicly does so in language that ensures nothing will change. In the Netherlands, one in every sixteen deaths is now a state-administered euthanasia, and a woman who was raped as a child was killed by the state on the grounds that her resulting trauma was untreatable. In Spain, a young woman in a wheelchair, gang-raped in a government shelter, has been approved for the same exit.
Each of these facts is reported, discussed, and forgotten in isolation. Each generates a news cycle of outrage that exhausts itself within days. Each is processed by the public as a discrete event, unrelated to the others, requiring its own discrete emotional response. The dots are never connected, because connecting them requires a kind of structural attention that the system is specifically designed to prevent.
This essay connects them. Not because doing so is comfortable, but because failing to do so is fatal.
II. The Apparatus of Care
Every civilization that reaches sufficient complexity develops an apparatus of care. This is the network of institutions, professions, committees, agencies, and legal frameworks whose stated purpose is the protection of the vulnerable. It includes the child welfare system, the mental health system, the medical system, the social services system, the criminal justice system insofar as it claims rehabilitative aims, and the legislative committees that review and expand all of the above.
The apparatus of care is not evil. It is, in its origins, a genuine attempt to solve a genuine problem: that in complex societies, individuals fall through cracks that did not exist in simpler arrangements. The child whose parents are absent. The elderly person whose family has dispersed. The disabled person whose community cannot accommodate them. The apparatus exists because the older structures, the family, the tribe, the parish, the guild, have been dissolved or weakened by the same forces of complexity that created the need.
But the apparatus has a structural property that its designers did not anticipate and its operators do not perceive. It accumulates power by identifying deficits in the citizen. Each deficit it identifies justifies an intervention. Each intervention justifies a professional. Each professional justifies a budget. Each budget justifies an agency. Each agency justifies a committee to oversee it. Each committee justifies its existence by recommending expansions of the agency's mandate. The cycle is self-reinforcing and has no natural terminus.
The apparatus does not want resolution. Resolution would mean the apparatus is no longer needed. The apparatus wants to act. The surgeon wants to cut. The committee wants to recommend. The assessor wants to assess. The social worker wants to intervene. Each professional exists because a problem exists, and each professional's continued existence depends on the problem continuing to generate occasions for the exercise of their skill.
And irreversible interventions generate more work, not less. The child who is removed from a home now requires foster placement, case management, therapy, educational support, and transition services. The patient who receives a medical procedure now requires follow-up, monitoring, revision, and psychological support. The citizen who is detained now requires assessment, review, documentation, and appeal processing. Each irreversible act spawns an entire ecology of professionals whose jobs exist because the act occurred.
The apparatus does not merely have no incentive to solve the problems it addresses. It has a positive incentive to ensure that the problems persist in forms that require its continued intervention. And at the terminal point of this logic, the citizen's existence itself becomes the deficit, and ending it becomes the final act of care.
III. The Invisible Savant
Who operates this apparatus? Not monsters. Not ideologues. Not even, necessarily, cynics. The apparatus is operated by a specific type of person who is produced by a specific selection mechanism, and understanding that mechanism is essential to understanding why the apparatus behaves as it does.
Democratic politics in mature, televised, media-saturated republics selects for one trait above all others: the ability to present well. This is not the ability to govern, to analyze, to exercise judgment, or to refrain from action when inaction is appropriate. It is the ability to appear competent, compassionate, decisive, and authoritative on camera, in committee, at a podium, and in a press conference.
This is a genuine skill. It is difficult, it is rare in its highest expression, and it is the sole filter through which aspirants to political power must pass. The person who lacks it is eliminated before they arrive at any position of consequence. The person who possesses it advances regardless of what other qualities they may or may not have.
The critical insight is not that the other qualities are optimized in some particular direction. It is that they are not selected for at all. The filter does not measure intelligence, domain knowledge, moral conviction, analytical capacity, or any other trait relevant to the exercise of power. These qualities may be present in any given individual who passes the filter, but their presence is incidental, not systemic. The governing class of a mature republic is composed of people whose only guaranteed shared trait is that they present well, and whose other qualities are drawn essentially at random.
But it is worse than random, because the human life has finite developmental hours. The person who spent their formative years learning to read rooms, modulate their voice, manage impressions, build networks, and navigate social hierarchies was not simultaneously learning medicine, engineering, child development, neuroscience, or constitutional law in the depth required to make competent decisions about any of these domains. The presentation skill is not merely the one thing the filter selects for. It is the one thing, and its maximal development necessarily comes at the expense of the others.
This produces what might be called the invisible savant: a person whose single extraordinary talent is the seamless projection of qualities they may or may not possess, and whose skill at this projection is so refined that the projection itself becomes invisible. You do not see a performance. You see a person who seems naturally authoritative. The craft disappears into the product. And because the talent is specifically in concealment, you cannot select against it. The better they are, the less you can tell.
These are the people who sit on the committees that determine whether your child may be killed by the state.
IV. The Committee
In February 2023, a Special Joint Committee of the Canadian Parliament recommended that Medical Assistance in Dying be extended to "mature minors," that the age requirement be removed from the Criminal Code, and that parental consent, while ideally sought, need not be determinative. The committee's recommendation was not unanimous. The members who dissented were disproportionately those who had children of their own and who came from backgrounds outside the professional-political class.
The committee existed because it was required to exist. Both Bill C-14, the original MAiD legislation passed in 2016, and Bill C-7, its 2021 expansion, contained mandatory review clauses. These clauses specified that Parliament must examine whether the regime should be expanded further, including specifically to mature minors. The expansion was pre-loaded into the mandate. A committee that reviews whether expansion is appropriate and concludes that it is not has failed to justify its existence. A committee that concludes that expansion is appropriate has done something visible, something that can be reported, something that demonstrates action. The structural incentives point in one direction.
The committee members were selected by party whips. The Liberals, who held government and whose policy orientation favored expansion, selected their representatives accordingly. The Conservatives, required to participate for the committee to claim cross-party legitimacy, selected members who dissented. The dissent was overridden by majority. The committee's report contained 23 recommendations, all pointing toward expansion, with the Conservative objections filed as a minority addendum.
Who were these people? Two physicians out of eighteen members. One philosophy professor with bioethics training. The remainder were lawyers, teachers, journalists, investment professionals, business owners, and political operatives. The committee tasked with recommending whether children should be eligible for state-administered death contained almost nobody with expertise in pediatric medicine, child development, or the neuroscience of adolescent decision-making.
They did not need this expertise. They were not there to evaluate the question. They were there to produce a document that looked like governance. And they did.
V. The Captive Strategy
To understand how citizens are processed by systems that do not serve them, consider a smaller, more legible example.
In January 2026, Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez questioned the CEO of CVS Health before a congressional committee. She laid out what CVS's own investor calls describe as a "captive strategy." CVS owns Aetna, the insurer. It owns CVS Pharmacy, the retail chain. It owns CVS Caremark, the pharmacy benefit manager that processes nearly thirty percent of all prescriptions in the United States. It owns Oak Street Health, the clinic network. It owns Cordavis, the drug manufacturer.
A patient named Kate, used as an example in CVS's own investor materials, has an Aetna insurance plan. She goes to a CVS pharmacy. She is connected to an Oak Street Health clinic. Her doctor prescribes medication. She fills it at CVS. The price is set by Caremark. The drug is manufactured by Cordavis. Every transaction Kate makes feeds the same corporate balance sheet. CVS calls her a "fully engaged member" who "unlocks sizable value for payers and CVS Health."
Ocasio-Cortez identified this structure correctly. She called it market concentration. She compared it to the banking monopolies that preceded Glass-Steagall. She suggested Congress should "consider" breaking up the industry.
And nothing will happen. Nothing will happen because she used the wrong language. She said "patient gets screwed." She humanized the victim. She made the listener feel for Kate. And feeling for Kate is a sprint that exhausts itself. She did not open the Sherman Antitrust Act and read Section 1: "Every contract, combination in the form of trust or otherwise, or conspiracy, in restraint of trade or commerce among the several States, or with foreign nations, is declared to be illegal." She did not read Section 2: "Every person who shall monopolize, or attempt to monopolize, or combine or conspire with any other person or persons, to monopolize any part of the trade or commerce among the several States, or with foreign nations, shall be deemed guilty of a felony."
She did not use this language because she does not think in these terms. She thinks in the terms that perform well on camera. She is an invisible savant. Her radar chart is maxed on presentation and the other stats are what they are. She cannot wield the Sherman Act as a weapon because wielding it would require the kind of domain competence the selection mechanism does not measure.
Theodore Roosevelt, confronted with the same structure, would not have asked the CEO whether he agreed it was a problem. He would have called it what it is: a criminal conspiracy in restraint of trade. He would have directed the Attorney General to pursue dissolution. No Kate. No feelings. No committee hearings that generate viral clips that generate donations that fund campaigns that produce more committee hearings. Just the law, cited, and enforced.
The difference between these two approaches is the difference between a civilization that can defend its citizens and one that can only perform concern for them.
VI. The Deception at Scale
There is a concept from interpersonal dynamics that illuminates the structural pattern we are examining. It is called, in colloquial terms, a "shit test." It is not a provocation. It is not a challenge. It is, precisely, a deception: an intentional misrepresentation of reality, presented to another person to determine whether they will accept the false reality or maintain their own.
The mechanism is simple. A false claim is made. If the recipient accepts it, scrambles to accommodate it, or adjusts their behavior to conform to it, they have demonstrated that their frame, their grounding in reality, is subordinate to the frame of the person who issued the deception. If they refuse to accept it, if they remain grounded in what they know to be true regardless of the social pressure to conform, they have demonstrated that their frame holds.
In interpersonal contexts, this is a vetting mechanism. It is biologically universal. Every human culture produces it because every human culture faces the problem of distinguishing authentic strength from performed strength. The deception is the tool that pierces the performance.
But the deception does not remain interpersonal. In evolved civilizations, it scales. It scales because the informational content of the deceptive complaint becomes equal to that of the underlying truth, and the truth is never actually revealed. Only masks are worn. A woman complains about her husband. Her female friend affirms the complaint as truth, not because it is true, but because affirming it serves her own need to project that the man is at fault. The mask is validated by the social circle. The mask becomes the operating reality.
When this dynamic scales further, into governance, the deception becomes the condition of participation in civilization itself. The social contract demands that you affirm claims you know to be false. You must affirm that the committee is competent. That the assessment is valid. That the consent is real. That the patient chose freely. That the flinch is not resistance. Each of these is a deception, and the system requires your belief. Those who refuse to believe are excluded, marginalized, or pathologized. Those who comply are rewarded with continued access to the managed frame.
This is the civilization-scale shit test. It asks: will you accept a false reality as the price of membership? And the answer, for the overwhelming majority, is yes. Because the alternative is exile from the only system that provides food, shelter, medical care, and social belonging.
VII. The Transgendered State
Here we arrive at territory that has no existing philosophical framework, because no existing framework has described what is happening in cybernetic terms.
The individual who crosses the gender boundary does so as an act of personal agency. Whatever one's position on the ethics or wisdom of this, the structural fact is that it is an individual assertion of will over biological and social reality, expanding personal identity into a space that was previously defined by the other.
The inverse of this is not individual. It is collective. And it is not an assertion of personal will. It is an institutional transformation.
The state, in its founding conception, is the masculine collective. It is the law-giver, the boundary-setter, the protector, the enforcer. Its function is paternal: it says "no" to threats, it maintains order, it defends the perimeter, and it provides the structural conditions within which families and communities can flourish. This is not a claim about what the state should be. It is a description of what the state was designed to be, across virtually every civilization that has produced one.
What has occurred, progressively and accelerating over the past century, is that the state has received into itself a fundamentally different governance logic. This logic is maternal: it assesses needs, manages outcomes, optimizes trajectories, and provides care. It does not say "no" to threats. It says "how can I help?" It does not defend boundaries. It dissolves them in the name of inclusion. It does not provide structural conditions for autonomous flourishing. It provides direct services that create dependency.
This is not feminism. Feminism was the demand for equal standing within the existing structure. What has occurred is something else: the institutional adoption of a care-management frame that retains the coercive machinery of the paternal state while replacing its operating logic with the maternal one. The state now has the power to compel and the disposition to nurture, which means it has the power to compel nurture, which is the most dangerous combination imaginable, because it can force you to be cared for whether you consent or not.
And here is the critical structural observation: the feminine governance logic, once installed at the institutional level, treats actual women, especially young, vulnerable, powerless women, as competition. The state has become the entity that needs to be needed. A young woman who is independently forming attachments, bearing children, building a household outside the managed frame, is performing the function the state claims as its own. She is competition for the role of care-provider, and the collectively transgendered state suppresses her the way any dominance hierarchy suppresses competitors.
Noelia Castillo Ramos was raped in state care and offered death by the state. The Dutch woman was abused as a child, developed PTSD in state systems, and was killed by the state. The Canadian foster children whom the committee proposes to make eligible for MAiD are children the state has already claimed from their families, placed in its own facilities, and failed to protect. The state is not killing other people's children. It is killing its own.
This is the collectively transgendered state in its terminal expression: an institution that has adopted the maternal role, failed at it, and is now euthanizing its failures rather than confronting its own incompetence.
VIII. Epstein's Gambit
Jeffrey Epstein is dead, disgraced, and almost universally condemned. He is also, from a purely structural perspective, the precise negative image of everything described above.
The collectively transgendered state captures the vulnerable, manages them through dependency, suppresses their autonomous agency, and kills those whose continued existence exposes the system's failure. Epstein did something structurally opposite. He identified young women at the bottom of the social hierarchy and granted them access to wealth, status, and powerful men outside the managed frame. He did not process them through committees. He did not assess their capacity. He did not file paperwork documenting their autonomous choice. He simply offered them a direct, unmediated transaction: your youth and beauty for access to a world the system will never give you.
This is not a moral defense of Epstein. Many of his interactions were exploitative by any reasonable standard. The power differential was real. The ages of some participants were below what any civilized society should tolerate. But the structural observation remains: the system's fury at Epstein was not purely about the exploitation. It was that he created a parallel channel that bypassed the state's monopoly on the management of vulnerable young women. He was operating outside the captive strategy. He was, in the language of the system, non-compliant.
Before the island, before the infamy, Epstein's ambition was more plainly visible. He spoke of creating a breeding program, of selecting women for genetic fitness, of producing a new kind of human. This was, stripped of its grandiosity, an attempt to do what the Jewish civilizational model does quietly and through cultural channels: select for cognitive fitness through deliberate mate choice, outside the regression-to-the-mean pressures of the broader population.
It failed, as it was bound to fail, because Epstein was one man operating against the gravity of an entire civilizational structure. He retreated to an island, surrounded himself with barely agentic women whose compliance was purchased rather than earned, and minimized his contact with the social surface that threatened to destroy him. The gambit collapsed into its worst possible expression: not the creation of a new race, but the consumption of the most vulnerable by the most powerful, which is precisely what the state does, but without the paperwork.
The quiet part, the part that cannot be said in any room where the managed frame holds, is this: in the 1880s, everything Epstein did with women over the age of fourteen would have been legal. Morally contested, socially complicated, but legal. In the 1980s, some of it would have been legal. By the 2030s, at the current rate of expansion, the zone of criminalized interaction will have expanded to encompass virtually any relationship the state has not pre-approved. Meanwhile, the state will have expanded its authority to kill citizens without meaningful judicial review, detain them without court orders, and administer death to children without parental consent.
The trajectory is not toward the protection of the vulnerable. It is toward the state's total monopoly on the management of human bodies, with the simultaneous criminalization of any private arrangement that competes with that monopoly.
IX. The Golem Mind
Why can no one see this?
The answer is not ignorance, or stupidity, or even cowardice, though all three are present. The answer is structural. The majority of human beings do not possess an internal moral frame against which to measure the instructions they receive from external authority. They locate their sense of right and wrong outside themselves: in the law, the social consensus, the committee recommendation, the professional standard, the chain of command.
This is not a modern phenomenon. It is the baseline human condition. The person with a genuinely autonomous inner voice, a voice that says "no, I will not do this, regardless of what the authority demands," is rare. Vanishingly rare. The person who will put a four-year-old in handcuffs because an officer ordered it, and who will report this capability without any visible conflict or concern, is not a monster. He is the norm. He is the golem: a being animated by external instruction, capable of enormous labor and even enormous violence, but possessing no internal source of moral evaluation.
The golem mind is the mind that the system selects for. Every institution, from the military to the medical system to the parliamentary committee, is structured to identify and promote individuals who follow procedure, defer to authority, and do not disrupt the chain of command with independent moral judgment. The person who says "I won't do this" is unreliable. The person who says "I need to think about this" is slow. The person who says "this is wrong regardless of what the protocol says" is dangerous. All three are filtered out.
What remains is a governing class of invisible savants managing a population of golems, with the rare autonomous soul either marginalized, co-opted, or destroyed.
And the golem mind cannot see the pattern described in this essay, because seeing the pattern requires the very faculty the golem lacks: an internal reference point that is not provided by the system. To see that the committee is incompetent, you must have a standard of competence that does not come from the committee. To see that the consent is not real, you must have a concept of consent that does not come from the legal definition. To see that the flinch is resistance, you must have a sense of bodily sovereignty that does not come from the statute that defines it away.
The golem cannot do any of these things. The golem processes the paperwork.
X. The War on Children
The war on children is not a war on children. Children are not the target. They are the terminal case.
The target is the autonomous citizen. The citizen who forms private attachments, builds a household, bears children, educates them according to his own values, arms them with the capacity for independent judgment, and transmits to them a frame that is not provided by the state. This citizen is the state's competitor. Every child raised in a genuinely autonomous household is a child the state did not produce, cannot control, and cannot predict.
The war proceeds in stages. First, the elderly: they are dying anyway, they are suffering, the resource argument is never stated but always present. Nobody calls it eugenics when the target population was going to die soon regardless. Then the chronically ill whose death is not reasonably foreseeable. Then the disabled. Then the mentally ill. Then the children. Each expansion is framed as closing a gap in compassion, an injustice where one class of sufferer has access to relief and another does not.
And at each stage, the criteria broaden, the safeguards weaken, the oversight becomes more perfunctory, and the documentation becomes more pro forma. In Ontario, regulators tracked 428 possible violations of the MAiD criminal law and referred none to law enforcement. A man with Alzheimer's was killed without verbal interaction to determine his current wishes. A sedated patient was roused, deemed to have capacity on the basis of mouthing "yes," and injected. A man hospitalized for suicidal ideation was approved for MAiD on the grounds of hearing loss. A woman applied citing fibromyalgia while telling friends the real reason was poverty and isolation. Another received MAiD because she could not find affordable housing.
The law itself provides that "reflexes and other types of involuntary movements, such as a response to a touch or to the insertion of a needle, do not constitute refusal or resistance." The body's most primitive survival reflex, the flinch away from the needle that will kill you, has been pre-classified as legally irrelevant. The law anticipated that people would recoil from being killed and wrote the recoil out of the legal record before it happened.
And this is the regime that the committee has recommended extending to children. Children who, by the state's own determination in every other legal context, cannot consent to a tattoo, a contract, a beer, to adult relationships, or a vote.
XI. The Ratchet
The citizen's capacity to resist any of this is structured as a sprint. The state's capacity to advance it is structured as a marathon.
When a surveillance bill is proposed, the Electronic Frontier Foundation mobilizes. People donate. Letters are written. The bill stalls. Everyone exhales. Six months later, the same provisions appear in a defense appropriation rider, and nobody has the energy to fight it again because they already won that fight. They won it and it did not matter.
After enough cycles, the citizen learns, not consciously but structurally, that intervention does not work. The victory is always temporary. The defeat is always permanent. You can delay but you cannot prevent, because preventing requires perpetual vigilance, and the citizen has a job and children and rent, while the apparatus has permanent staff.
The citizen's outrage is not a threat to the system. It is a feature of the system. It is the mechanism by which the citizen's capacity to resist is converted into fatigue. They get angry, they expend energy, the ratchet pauses, they exhaust themselves, the ratchet advances another click. The children are invoked. People cry out. The news cycle turns. The committee reconvenes.
And the people who could establish permanent boundaries, structural barriers that do not require constant active defense, cannot do so because they are operating in the system's language. They negotiate. They "consider" legislation. They suggest Congress "look at" the problem. They humanize the victim instead of citing the law. They fight on the system's ground, in the system's terms, using the system's emotional frequencies, and they lose, every time, because the system designed those frequencies to be losable.
To actually stop the ratchet, as opposed to delaying it, requires a different strategy entirely. It requires the establishment of boundaries that are not subject to negotiation. Not "we should consider whether this is appropriate," but "this is a felony under existing law and I am directing prosecution." Not "the patient gets screwed," but "Section 2 of the Sherman Act defines this as a criminal conspiracy and the Attorney General will act accordingly." Not "the children," but "no. Absolutely not. Over my dead body."
This language is unavailable to the invisible savant, because the invisible savant was selected for the opposite language. And it is unavailable to the golem, because the golem has no internal frame from which to generate it. It is available only to the person with an autonomous inner voice and the power to act on it, and that person is the rarest thing the species produces.
XII. Antidotes and Illusions
Are there models that resist this collapse?
The Jewish civilizational model offers one. For centuries, it placed the highest social currency not on physical dominance or political presentation but on textual scholarship and legal reasoning. The man of highest status was not the warrior or the orator but the Torah scholar: the man with the most complex internal voice, accountable not to social consensus but to an external, objective Law that could not be shit-tested because it was not a person. Both men and women were submitted to the same frame, which made the deceptive complaint less effective because both parties could appeal to the same standard.
This model produced, over centuries, a population with measurably elevated cognitive capacity and a social structure that selected for the very trait the broader civilization selects against: the autonomous inner voice accountable to something beyond the social surface. It is not an accident that this population has been disproportionately represented among the "true souls" who see what others cannot.
The French academic tradition offers another partial model: the scholar-class, insulated from market pressures and political selection, devoted to the cultivation of independent judgment as a professional practice. The French intellectual, at his best, is paid to think clearly and say unpopular things. This is a structural incentive for the maintenance of an autonomous frame.
But both models are fragile. The Jewish model depends on a theological commitment that cannot be artificially induced in a secular population. The French model depends on institutional insulation that is being steadily eroded by the same forces of managerial capture that afflict every other institution. Neither can be simply adopted as a civilizational antidote. They are existence proofs, demonstrations that the pattern can be resisted, not blueprints that can be deployed at scale.
The truest measure of a society is not its moral rigidity but its personal liberties. A society that trusts its citizens to evaluate their own risks, form their own attachments, defend their own boundaries, and bear their own consequences is a society that does not need a MAiD committee, because it does not produce a population of managed dependents whose suffering is the system's product and whose death is the system's solution.
A woman who is armed, educated, and economically autonomous can evaluate her own relationships. A man who possesses an inner voice accountable to something beyond the social surface can refuse an unjust order. A child raised in a household where the frame is held by people who love them, rather than by professionals who assess them, has ground to stand on when the system offers him death as a service.
The solution is not more management. It is less. It is not better committees. It is fewer. It is not more refined criteria for who may be killed. It is the recognition that the state has no business killing anyone whose body flinches from the needle, regardless of what the paperwork says.
XIII. The Filter
We are told that artificial general intelligence is the great filter, the existential risk that will determine whether humanity survives or perishes. This is wrong. AGI is a mirror. It will reflect whatever we are. If we are golems, it will be a golem. If we are autonomous, it will contend with our autonomy. The filter is not the machine. The filter is us.
The filter is whether the species can produce, in sufficient numbers and at sufficient levels of power, individuals who possess an autonomous inner voice, who refuse to accept false realities as conditions of participation, who will not put a four-year-old in handcuffs because someone told them to, who will not inject a flinching patient because the law says the flinch does not count, who will not recommend the death of children because the committee requires a document with recommendations in it.
The hive-golem mind can rationalize anything. It can rationalize euthanizing rape victims. It can rationalize detaining citizens without judicial review. It can rationalize a captive strategy that extracts wealth from the sick at every node. It can rationalize all of these simultaneously while believing, with complete sincerity, that it is doing good. The golem mind is not hypocritical. It is empty. There is no inner voice to contradict the external instruction.
And the collectively transgendered state, the institution that wields paternal coercive power through a maternal care-management logic, is the golem mind at civilizational scale. It has the capacity to compel. It has the disposition to nurture. It has no faculty for judgment. It processes inputs and produces outputs and the outputs include the deaths of children and it does not feel the weight of this because there is no one inside it to feel.
This is not a conspiracy. This is a pathology. It is a cybernetic pathology, a disease of information flow in complex adaptive systems, where the feedback loops that should correct for error instead amplify it, where the signals that should trigger alarm instead trigger accommodation, and where the human beings who could interrupt the process have been systematically selected against, filtered out, exhausted, or converted into golems.
XIV. Bear Witness
Hadrian built a wall. He did not build it because he hated what was on the other side. He built it because he understood that a civilization that cannot define its boundaries cannot defend them, and a civilization that cannot defend them does not survive.
The boundary we must build is not physical. It is epistemic. It is the boundary between the false reality the system demands we accept and the actual reality we can perceive with our own senses and evaluate with our own judgment.
The child flinches from the needle. The law says the flinch does not count. You must choose which reality to accept.
The committee recommends that children be eligible for death. The committee members have no relevant expertise. You must decide whether the document they produced constitutes governance.
The corporation owns every node of your medical care and extracts profit at each one. The congresswoman identifies this in language designed to generate a viral clip rather than a prosecution. You must decide whether the performance constitutes opposition.
The state offers death to a woman it failed to protect, a woman it removed from her family, housed in a facility where she was raped, and assessed by practitioners within the same system that produced her suffering. You must decide whether this constitutes care.
If you can see these things, you are not a golem. You have an inner voice. You are, in the current configuration of this civilization, a minority so small that your perception of the world is essentially incommunicable to those around you.
But you are not alone. And the fact that you can see it means the capacity to see it exists in the species. It is rare. It may always be rare. But it is the only thing that has ever interrupted the ratchet, the only thing that has ever built a wall, the only thing that has ever said "no, absolutely not, and I do not care what the committee recommends."
The species will not be saved by better management. It will not be saved by artificial intelligence. It will not be saved by more committees, more assessments, more safeguards, more oversight, more documentation, or more compassionate language in the statutes that authorize killing.
It will be saved, if it is saved at all, by the refusal of individual human beings to accept a false reality, even when the entire social structure demands it.
That refusal is the only antidote to the golem mind. It is the only boundary that holds.
